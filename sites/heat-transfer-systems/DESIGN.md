# Heat Transfer Systems: art direction

**Read:** a St. Louis ASME Code fabricator of heat exchangers, pressure vessels and waste-heat recovery equipment, founded 1980. Real fabrication photography (banners) and a drilling video exist. It should feel like an industrial photo essay: big real photos and terse captions.

- **Type:** Chivo (display and body, 800 for h1/h2, 400 body) with Chivo Mono (photo captions, data plates, small labels).
- **Palette** (from the logo and banners): carbon `#231f20`, steel `#6d686a`, mill white `#f3f2f2`, warm silver `#d8d2d4`, and white `#ffffff` for the header. Carbon on white and white on carbon both contrast at 16.3:1; the carbon focus outline on white also contrasts at 16.3:1.
- **Hero:** a full-bleed photo. A real banner photo fills the viewport width at about 78vh with a bottom-weighted dark scrim (a solid-to-transparent black scrim, not a colored gradient). The h1 and actions sit bottom-left, with a mono caption plate bottom-right naming what the photo shows (only if it is known from the source; otherwise omit it).
- **Nav:** a solid white sticky header on every page, with the transparent dark logo, a Products dropdown, direct page links, a quiet phone link and a carbon Contact button. At narrower widths a Menu button opens the complete grouped navigation in a white panel.
- **Grid and rhythm:** 1240px max. Capabilities alternate full-bleed photo rows with narrow text columns. 120px sections.
- **Cards:** none. Use photo-led rows with mono index numbers.
- **Signature detail:** mono data-plate captions on photos, like a stamped equipment nameplate (1px warm silver border, uppercase mono).

## Logo

The original site displays the graphic monogram and stacked name from https://heattransfersys.com/img/HTS-square.png (108 × 120 pixels). The unmodified transparent original is saved at `img-originals/logo/original.png`; the displayed copy is upscaled 2× with Lanczos, preserving the full artwork and its transparency. The logo is displayed 88px tall at desktop widths and 62px tall on small screens. The opaque artwork is `#231f20`; the header places it directly on white, while the footer uses the same transparent artwork recolored white on carbon. The carbon palette uses that exact ink, with warm silver derived from its neutral hue for actions and rules.
